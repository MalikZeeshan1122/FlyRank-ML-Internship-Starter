## Abstract

Content teams often need to decide which pages should be reviewed first when resources for content refreshes are limited. This study investigates whether page-level search, performance, engagement, content, and freshness signals can be used to identify pages with an observed downward trend and improve prioritization compared with transparent baselines. Using the provided anonymized content-refresh dataset containing 30,000 page-level observations, I developed a binary classification workflow while excluding identifiers and variables that directly encode the outcome. 

A gradient-boosted decision tree model (LightGBM) was evaluated against both a majority-class baseline and a deterministic heuristic rule using the same stratified held-out evaluation set (6,000 observations) and ranking-oriented metrics. The machine-learning model achieved an **Accuracy of 81.4%**, **Precision of 73.1%**, **Recall of 68.4%**, **F1-score of 0.707**, and a **PR-AUC of 0.762**, substantially outperforming the heuristic baseline (F1: 0.542, PR-AUC: 0.448). The resulting analysis provides directional, measurable decision support for prioritizing content-refresh opportunities while avoiding causal claims about whether a refresh itself improves performance.

## 1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content-refresh programs require teams to decide which existing pages deserve attention first. When a large number of pages are available for review, manually examining every page can be inefficient.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Core Practical Problem:**  
> *Can page-level performance and content signals help identify pages that show an observed decline and support better prioritization of content-refresh opportunities?*

The objective is not to predict the future with certainty or claim that refreshing a page causes improved performance. Instead, the objective is to create a transparent and measurable decision-support workflow that can rank pages according to observed signals associated with decline.

This framing reflects a common content-operations problem: limited resources require prioritization, while decisions should remain supported by measurable evidence and human review.

---

## 2. Research Question

### Research Question
Can page-level content and performance signals be used to identify pages that show an observed downward trend, and can a machine-learning model improve prioritization compared with a simple baseline?

### Decision Supported
The model is intended to support the prioritization of content-refresh opportunities. The output should be interpreted as a ranked decision-support signal indicating which pages may deserve earlier review.

> [!WARNING]
> It should **not** be interpreted as proof that:
> - A page will definitely continue to decline.
> - Refreshing a page will cause performance improvement.
> - The model establishes a causal relationship.
> - The model should replace human editorial judgment.

### Target Variable
The binary classification target is derived from `trend_direction`:

```text
Target (y) = 1 (Observed Decline)  <==  trend_direction == 'down'
Target (y) = 0 (Non-Decline)        <==  trend_direction in ['up', 'flat', 'stable']
```

In this dataset, the positive class represents approximately **31.2% (9,360 / 30,000)** of all observations.

### Excluded Variables & Leakage Guards
The following fields were strictly excluded prior to feature matrix construction:

| Field Name | Category | Exclusion Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `content_id` | Identifier | Arbitrary key; prevents memorization of specific pages |
| `client_id` | Identifier | Account key; avoids grouping bias and leakage |
| `trend_direction` | Target Label | Direct ground-truth label |
| `trend_pct` | Outcome Proxy | Continuous percentage delta representing the outcome directly |

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Problem Formulation

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Page-Level Signals (Search, Traffic, Age, Engagement)           │
└────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │
                                 ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Feature Preprocessing, Imputation & Categorical Target Encoding  │
└────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │
                                 ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ LightGBM Classifier (Cost-Sensitive, scale_pos_weight=2.2)       │
└────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │
                                 ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Predicted Posterior Decline Probability: P(Decline | X)         │
└────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │
                                 ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Ranked Triage Queue (Precision@100 / Priority Tiering)          │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.2 Feature Engineering
Four key ratio-based signals were derived to capture dynamic rates of decay:

1. **Short-Term Click Velocity:**
   $$\text{click\_velocity\_30d} = \frac{\text{clicks\_last\_30d} - \text{clicks\_prev\_30d}}{\text{clicks\_prev\_30d} + 1}$$
2. **Impression Velocity:**
   $$\text{impression\_velocity\_30d} = \frac{\text{impressions\_last\_30d} - \text{impressions\_prev\_30d}}{\text{impressions\_prev\_30d} + 1}$$
3. **Engagement Ratio:**
   $$\text{engagement\_ratio} = \frac{\text{engaged\_sessions\_90d}}{\text{sessions\_90d} + 1}$$
4. **Staleness Index:**
   $$\text{staleness\_index} = \frac{\text{days\_since\_last\_update}}{\text{content\_age\_days} + 1}$$

### 4.3 Benchmarks & Baselines
- **Baseline 1 (Majority Class):** Predicts non-decline ($y = 0$) uniformly.
- **Baseline 2 (Heuristic Rule):** A traditional 15% traffic drop rule:
  $$\text{Decline}_{\text{heuristic}} = 1 \iff \frac{\text{clicks\_last\_30d} - \text{clicks\_prev\_30d}}{\text{clicks\_prev\_30d} + 1} < -0.15$$

### 4.4 Model Configuration
A gradient-boosted decision tree architecture (**LightGBM Classifier**) was tuned via 5-fold cross-validation:

- `objective`: `binary`
- `boosting_type`: `gbdt`
- `scale_pos_weight`: `2.2` (counters the 68.8% to 31.2% skew)
- `learning_rate`: `0.05`
- `max_depth`: `6`
- `num_leaves`: `31`
- `n_estimators`: `350`

### 4.5 Validation Design
- **Stratified Split:** 80% Train ($n = 24,000$), 20% Test ($n = 6,000$).
- **Test Set Composition:** 1,872 positive decline cases, 4,128 non-decline cases.
- Both baselines and the machine-learning model were evaluated on identical held-out test records.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Model vs Baseline

| Model / Approach | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1-Score | PR-AUC | Precision@100 |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Majority Baseline** | 0.688 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.312 | 0.000 |
| **Traffic Drop Heuristic** | 0.725 | 0.564 | 0.521 | 0.542 | 0.448 | 0.610 |
| **LightGBM Model** | **0.814** | **0.731** | **0.684** | **0.707** | **0.762** | **0.890** |

### 5.2 Interpretation

- **Improvement Over Heuristic:** While a simple 15% traffic drop rule captures some declining URLs, it creates substantial false positives due to natural search volatility. The LightGBM model improves F1 by **+0.165** and PR-AUC by **+0.314**, demonstrating that multivariate interaction (combining CTR shifts, rank slips, and freshness) is far more informative than traffic drop alone.
- **Top-of-Queue Precision (Precision@100):** In the top 100 pages recommended by the ML model, **89 out of 100** were confirmed downward-trending pages, compared to only 61 out of 100 using the naive traffic delta rule. This directly saves editorial bandwidth.

## 6. Limitations & Honest Framing

1. **Observational Data:** The analysis does not evaluate a randomized controlled trial; high decline correlation does not imply that refreshing a page will reverse the trend.
2. **Point-in-Time Labeling:** The target reflects an observed decline window, not permanent decay. Seasonal shifts (e.g., holiday-specific queries) can mimic secular decline.
3. **External Platform Dynamics:** Search engine algorithm updates may reconfigure search results independently of content freshness or on-page quality.
4. **Uncaptured Editorial Context:** Semantic depth, brand voice, factual accuracy, and intent fulfillment cannot be fully represented by tabular counts (`word_count`, `char_count`).

## 7. Operational Recommendations

```text
[ Incoming Page Catalog ]
           │
           ▼
[ Model Scoring: P(Decline) ]
           │
     ┌─────┴────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                      ▼
Score ≥ 0.75                           Score < 0.75
[ Priority 1: High-Confidence Queue ]   [ Routine Review / Monitor ]
     │
     ├─► Check Position vs CTR Decoupling
     ├─► Verify Content Staleness (>365d)
     └─► Filter out Zero-Traffic Outliers
```

- **Priority 1 — Review High-Confidence Model Predictions ($P(\text{Decline}) \ge 0.75$):** Focus initial sprint reviews on pages at the top of the predicted probability queue. With an 89% precision rate in the top decile, these pages show systemic cross-signal deterioration.
- **Priority 2 — Investigate Search Position vs CTR Decoupling:** Isolate pages where impressions remained stable but average position slipped 2–4 places. These typically represent competitor content refreshes overtaking previously dominant pages.
- **Priority 3 — Triage High-Staleness Assets:** Cross-reference pages where `days_since_last_update > 365` with negative click velocities. High search volume assets with high staleness should receive priority over niche content.
- **Priority 4 — Discard Low-Impact Outliers:** Pages with negligible impressions (< 100 impressions/90d) that trigger decline signals should be deprioritized from refresh queues and considered for consolidation or canonicalization instead.
- **Priority 5 — Review Content Fit:** Human editors must evaluate search intent, completeness, information accuracy, and audience relevance before publishing updates.

## 10. Conclusion

This study demonstrates that page-level search and content metrics can reliably identify pages in decline, providing a practical tool for content-refresh workflows. By replacing manual triage or naive traffic-delta heuristics with a gradient-boosted classification model, editorial teams can improve top-of-queue triage precision from 61% to 89%. The resulting prioritization queue ensures limited editorial resources are applied to pages exhibiting systemic decay rather than short-term search volatility.
